## Step 1 — survey the field (do this first, before any writing)

Find apps a client or therapist might use to keep an audio journal with speech-to-text for
personal or therapy use, with some claim about secure storage. Cover at least these
categories, and name real products in each with a source: general voice-journal apps (e.g.
Day One with audio, Reflectly, Journey, Voicenotes, Otter-style transcription used as a
journal), therapy-adjacent journaling apps (mood/CBT journals with voice), therapist–client
portal products that include client journaling (e.g. practice-management tools with client
apps), and Apple's own Journal app. Aim for 8–12 apps. For each, record: platform(s);
recording in-app; transcription (on-device / cloud / none; which engine if stated); whether
the audio is kept as the source of truth or discarded after transcription; where data is
stored (device only / vendor cloud / user's own iCloud or Drive); encryption claims (at rest,
in transit, end-to-end — quote the vendor); account required; who else can read it
(vendor staff, AI-training clauses in the privacy policy — quote); therapist sharing model
(none / export / shared portal); export format (can the user take everything out?); price;
HIPAA or BAA claims (quote exactly; note when a claim is absent). Cite every cell. Where you
cannot verify, write "not stated" — never infer.

## Step 2 — Raconte's column, from the repo (grep, do not guess)

Read `docs/overview.md` (refreshed 2026-09-07), `docs/export-format.md`, and
`docs/plans/2026-07-29-data-model-and-migration.md`. Facts to state, each verified in the
code: native iOS + macOS app; records in-app; on-device transcription (Apple
SpeechAnalyzer/SpeechTranscriber, iOS 26 — `Raconte/Transcription/`); **audio is the ground
truth and the transcript is a derived, replaceable interpretation** (the repo's first
principle); storage is the app's own container on the device plus the user's own iCloud
private database via CloudKit (`Raconte/Sync/`) — no Raconte server, no account, no
analytics, no third party; the developer cannot read a user's data; export is a
byte-for-byte archive package with a per-file checksum and an in-app verifier
(`Raconte/Export/`, About → Archive); nothing is auto-deleted — corrupt entries are
quarantined, never destroyed (`Raconte/Library/StagedRemoval.swift`); single user by design —
no therapist login, no shared portal; sharing with a therapist means the client chooses to
export and hand over. Status: private TestFlight, not on the App Store; iOS 26 / macOS 26
required. Do NOT claim HIPAA compliance, a BAA, or end-to-end encryption. The honest
sentence on encryption: data in the iCloud private database is encrypted by Apple in transit
and at rest; with Apple's Advanced Data Protection turned on, Apple itself cannot read it.

## Step 3 — the two pages

Audience: a therapist, not a developer. Plain words, short sentences, no jargon without a
one-line gloss. Voice: the repo's docs are "plain and terse, no grandiose prose" — match it.

Page 1: a one-paragraph "what it is" (spoken journaling, the recording is what is kept, the
text is a helper); "what it is for" in therapy terms (between-session reflection, the client
owns the record, nothing is shared unless the client chooses); the privacy section titled
"Where your words go" in five or six plain statements, each true per Step 2, ending with the
single caveat about Advanced Data Protection and the explicit line that Raconte is not a
medical device and makes no HIPAA claim; "what it is not" (no therapist portal, no accounts,
not in the App Store yet).

Page 2: the matrix — Raconte as the first column, then the surveyed apps, rows for the Step 1
fields that matter to a therapist (where stored / who can read it / audio kept / on-device
transcription / account required / export / therapist sharing / HIPAA claim / price). Use
short cell values (Yes / No / Vendor cloud / Own iCloud / Not stated). A footnote line with
"Checked <date>; sources in the repo's survey.md". Below the matrix, three or four lines on
how a client would share with a therapist today (export → hand over the package or a
transcript.md) and a one-line contact.
