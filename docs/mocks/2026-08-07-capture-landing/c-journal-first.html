The physical metaphor: pick a journal off the shelf, open it, work in it. The home screen is the shelf (which is the journal switcher — issue #18 becomes a whole designed surface, not a menu). A journal's home holds its entries, its record button, and its settings — including per-journal delete protection (#35) and the two-voices default. There is no separate Library and no Recents: the journal's own entry list is both. Capture is one level deeper and carries its journal in the breadcrumb.

What this variant claims

  • Journal focus is a place, not a setting. You are always “in” exactly the journal whose pages you're looking at; there is nothing to keep in sync because there's only one journal on screen at a time.
  • One switcher, and it's the shelf. #18 stops being a menu problem: journals are large designed cards with cover, range, count, and their two-voices / lock tags.
  • #35 gets a natural home: per-journal Settings (jump pill 2 → Settings) carries the Easy / Confirm / Locked delete friction and the two-voices default.
  • Recents dissolves. After a capture you land back on the journal's own list with the new entry highlighted on top — the list is the receipt.
  • Move-to-journal = the entry walks to another shelf slot and you follow it: landing is the destination journal's home (jump pill 6).

Marker reachability

  • Same fixed thumb bar as A and B: voice · stop · ¶ glued to the bottom edge, nothing scrolls out from under your thumb.

Costs this variant pays (honest)

  • Present-day quick capture is two taps deeper (shelf → journal → record) unless the app reopens on the last journal home — mocked here: “Shelf” is where you start only on first launch.
  • Cross-journal browsing/search needs the separate “All entries” surface; the flat one-timeline view stops being the default (user-journeys §6 wants it back at reread time).
  • Interruption/resume, per-journal backdate carry, and two-voices gating behave exactly as shipped.
